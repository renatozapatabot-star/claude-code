# THE THREE DISCIPLINES

Epictetus did not teach maxims. He taught three fields of training. Everything else in this book — every drill, every weapon against anger or grief or sloth — belongs to one of the three. Learn to name which discipline a failure came from and you will stop treating symptoms.

> There are three areas of study in which a person who is going to be good and noble must be trained. The first concerns desires and aversions, so that he may never fail to get what he desires, nor fall into what he would avoid. The second concerns motives to act and not to act, and, in general, appropriate action: that he should act in an orderly fashion, with good reason, and not carelessly. The third is concerned with the avoidance of error and rashness in judgement.
>
> — *Discourses* III.2.1–2

He put desire first because that is where the student is bleeding. He put judgement last because it is hardest, and because it governs the other two. Train in his order. Do not skip to the one that flatters you.

---

## I. DESIRE AND AVERSION

*What you want, and what you refuse.*

You suffer because you demand from the world what the world never agreed to give, and flee what no one has been permitted to avoid. The cure is not to want less. It is to move desire off what is not yours.

One thing is good: excellence of character. One thing is evil: its absence. Everything else — health, money, reputation, office, the body, the people you love — is indifferent. Not worthless: most of it is to be preferred, pursued, protected. But it is material, not measure. The man who calls these things good has handed the verdict on his life to a fever, a market, a stranger's mood.

So: prefer everything, demand nothing.

> Seek not that the things which happen should happen as you wish; but wish the things which happen to be as they are, and you will have a tranquil flow of life.
>
> — *Enchiridion* VIII

That is not consolation. It is the hardest sentence in the book. It does not say *accept what you cannot change*. It says will it. Wish for it. The event you are dreading, should it arrive, is to be met by a man who wanted exactly that.

**The drill.** Name what you are demanding of today. Downgrade it, aloud, from demand to preference. State what you will do if it does not come. Then pursue it as hard as if you had demanded it — harder, because now nothing is riding on it.

**The failure.** You got the thing and were not improved. You lost it and collapsed. Both prove the desire was pointed outside the wall.

## II. ACTION

*What you do, and what you owe.*

You did not choose your part. You choose only how it is played.

> Remember that thou art an actor in a play, the character of which is determined by the poet: if he wishes the play to be short, it is short; if long, it is long: if he wishes you to act the part of a poor man, see that you act the part naturally; if the part of a lame man, of a magistrate, of a private person, do the same. For this is your duty, to act well the part that is given to you; but to select the part belongs to another.
>
> — *Enchiridion* XVII

You are a man, and so owe reason. A citizen, and so owe justice. A son, a friend, a worker, perhaps a father — each role names a debt, and the debt does not wait for the other party to deserve it. Justice is not a mood you arrive at. It is the content of the role, performed whether or not it is witnessed, returned, or recorded.

Every act goes out under one condition, spoken in advance: *I will do this, if nothing prevents me.* Then the obstacle, when it comes, finds your will already in agreement, and no failure of the world can be converted into a failure of yours.

**The drill.** Name the roles you hold today. Name the single duty each one is owed. Do the one you are avoiding before noon. Once this week, do something useful for a person who cannot repay you and will not know it was you.

**The failure.** You performed the role for the audience instead of performing it. Applause is the tell.

## III. ASSENT

*What you believe, and what you refuse to sign.*

Nothing that happens to you has yet said anything about you. The impression arrives on its own; the verdict is added by you, and it is the verdict that wounds.

> Men are disturbed not by the things which happen, but by the opinions about the things.
>
> — *Enchiridion* V

The first movement is not in your power. The heart jumps, the face heats, the mind offers its picture of ruin. You are not judged for that. You are judged for what you do in the half-second after.

> Straightway then practise saying to every harsh appearance, *You are an appearance, and in no manner what you appear to be.*
>
> — *Enchiridion* I.5

Strip the event to what a camera would have recorded. A man said these words and left the room. There is a number on a page. A body is in a bed. Everything beyond that — the betrayal, the catastrophe, the verdict on your worth — is commentary you are writing and then reading back as news.

**The drill.** Three questions, in this order. What happened? What did I add? Is the addition mine to keep? Then withhold assent until you can answer the third without flinching.

**The failure.** You signed instantly and called it perception. Every strong opinion you formed in under a second today was this.

## THE AUDIT

The three disciplines produce one thing or they are wasted: a good character. Measure it, or you are only reading.

At the end of any disturbance, and at the end of the day, four questions:

Was it **wise** — did I see what was there, or what I feared and wanted?
Was it **just** — did I give each person what was owed?
Was it **courageous** — did I do the thing I did not want to do?
Was it **temperate** — did I take the right amount and stop?

A no is not an occasion for shame. Shame is a way of feeling productive while changing nothing. A no is a location: desire, action, or assent. Go train there tomorrow.

You will fail at all three today. The measure of a student is not the failure. It is the interval — how long you stayed wrong before you came back. Shorten the interval. That is the entire art.

Miss once and you are a man who missed. Miss twice and you have begun to be a man who quits. Do not become him.
