# THE OPEN HAND

### The Handbook of Renato Zapata IV

Epictetus never wrote a book. A student wrote his lines down  
and called it the thing you keep in your hand.  
This one is yours.

It exists to make you a true Stoic,  
and your life the most beautiful thing you build.

However you found this page — steady or on fire — you are breathing.

Breathe in, close the fist. That is full force — everything you will give.  
Breathe out, open the hand. That is open hand — everything you cannot hold.

Two words. Two breaths. Every time they come back, your hand goes with them.

**Full force. Open hand.**

---

## THE CARD

*One screen. Ten seconds. Learn this page first.*

> "There are more things, Lucilius, likely to frighten us  
> than there are to crush us; we suffer more often  
> in imagination than in reality."  
> — Seneca, *Letters* 13.4

Imagination is not evidence.

Some things are yours. Most things are not.  
**Not mine. Return.**

The obstacle is the training.

Given back, not lost.

The feast is still on the table.

Fist in. Open out.  
**Full force. Open hand.**

On fire? Turn the page — breathe on the way.

---

## THE FOUR BEATS

*One exhale per line.*

1. **BREATHE** — longer out than in.
2. **NAME** — only what a camera would catch.
3. **SORT** — yours, or not yours?
4. **ACT** — one move. Take it.

Flooded? Use your own name:  
*Renato, what is the fact? What is yours now?*

### THE SORT

> "Some things are in our control and others not."  
> — Epictetus, *The Handbook* 1

Yours: judgment, aim, word, act. Own them.  
Shared: the body, the money, the work, other people.  
The effort is yours. The result never was.  
Give everything. Count on nothing.  
Not yours: the past, another man's will, every outcome.  
Stop arguing with closed doors.

**Not mine. Return.**

---

## ON FIRE

*You came here fast. Breathe out longer than you breathed in —*  
*fist in, open out.*  
*Spinning. Anger. Craving. Grief. Stuck. Empty. Beats first. Always.*

### SPINNING

The same scene, tenth showing.  
A funeral for something that has not died.

1. Find a fact you could photograph.
2. No fact? Say it once, aloud: **imagination is not evidence.**
3. Decide now. When the movie restarts:  
   *Seen it. No new evidence. Back to the room.*

Fills the sky? That's not its size. That's how close you're standing.  
Back up until it looks small. Then act.

### ANGER

Heat in the face.  
The speech already drafting itself.

1. Say nothing for one full breath.
2. What does this cost my character?  
   Nothing — unless I sign. **Not mine. Return.**
3. Line crossed? Choose a consequence  
   you can enforce calmly.

> "The best revenge is not to be like that."  
> — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 6.6

If you are not safe, leave first. Distance is not defeat.

### CRAVING

The glow around a thing  
that goes ordinary once you hold it.

1. Strip it plain. Ten words, no glamour.
2. Wait ten minutes. Cravings crest and fall. All of them.
3. Feed the real hunger — rest, company, a break.

**Not never. Not now by reflex.**

Pleasure is a great guest and a terrible boss.

### GRIEF

The wave. Let it through.  
Crying is not a failure — this hurts because it mattered.

1. Stand still until the wave passes.
2. Say the oldest words:

   > "Never say about anything, 'I have lost it,'  
   > but say, 'I have restored it.' ..."  
   > — Epictetus, *The Handbook* 11

3. Do one act of care for someone still here.  
   Grief moves when love has somewhere to go.

Given back, not lost.

### STUCK

The task, grown larger than the day.

1. Shrink it to the next physical move:  
   the keystroke, the call, the door.
2. Begin badly. Beginning is the only door there is.

> "The impediment to action advances action.  
> What stands in the way becomes the way."  
> — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 5.20

The dread of the work was heavier than the work.

### EMPTY

The verdicts: nothing matters, everything is too much.  
That is a tired body doing philosophy.

1. Eat. Water. Shrink the next task.
2. Sleep before you sentence your whole life.
3. Still empty after rest? Help one person.

The feast is still on the table.

---

If the dark stays for weeks, or you think of hurting yourself,  
tell someone tonight. Asking is the practice, not a break from it.

## THE HAND

*Three moves the Beats don't carry. Learn them once.*

Make a fist. Open three fingers, one at a time.

**Thumb — THE FEE.** Traffic, rudeness, delay:  
the fee for living among people. Paid. Drive.

**Index — REHEARSE THE LOSS.** Once a week,  
when the house goes quiet, close your eyes.  
Picture someone you love, gone. Fifteen seconds.  
Open them: still here.  
End on the hello, never on the loss.

**Middle — RETURN.** One breath, one line, back.

A closed fist cannot receive.

Fist in. Open out.  
**Full force. Open hand.**

---

## MORNING

*Ninety seconds. Ten seconds counts.*

When your feet touch the floor, before you stand,  
let the jaw unclench and say it:

> Some things are up to me. Most things are not.  
> Today I work my side of the line.

Then go meet the friction before it meets you:

> "When you wake up in the morning, tell yourself:  
> the people I deal with today will be meddling,  
> ungrateful, arrogant, dishonest, jealous, and surly."  
> — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 2.1

Not a prediction.  
You are taking its one weapon: surprise.

Before the first sip, write today's one aim.  
One line, where you'll see it again.  
Under it: *today, when the heat comes, I go quiet and finish.*

Then the first sip: three breaths before it.  
Heat, weight, bitterness.  
The feast is still on the table.

---

## THE BELLS

*The day rings them. You just answer.*

Door handle — *the fee. Already paid.*

Rehearsing an argument — *imagination or reality?* Run the Beats.

Waiting on a result — *it's pending. My life isn't.*  
Pick the day you'll check. Then put it down.

A hard call — *Am I ducking it, or overdoing it?*

Your doorway, coming home — one exhale.  
The day stays outside. Inside is the feast.

---

## EVENING

*When the phone touches the charger, this begins.*  
*Three minutes. One question counts.*

What did I do well?  
Where did I slip?  
Tomorrow, when ___ happens, I do ___ instead.  
Fill both blanks out loud.

Be honest, not cruel. Tonight, you are forgiven.

And what joy was handed to me?  
Name it — a face, a sound, a taste.

The last line before sleep is an inhale —  
by now you know what the exhale is for.

**The day is returned. My hand is open.**

---

## THE SEARCHLIGHT

You do not attract what you want.  
You become what you attend to —  
and what you become, you build.

> "The things you think about determine  
> the quality of your mind. Your soul  
> takes on the color of your thoughts."  
> — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 5.16

**Aim it.** The line you wrote this morning is the aim.  
Attention is a searchlight. Point it and you start seeing  
doors you walked past for years. They were always there.

**Believe it.** Believe you can and you try more,  
ask bigger, stay longer. The tries pile up.  
That is the whole secret, and it is work.

**See the work, not just the win.** Before the thing that matters —  
the call, the reps, the room — run it once in your head, done well.  
Sixty seconds. Dreaming of the trophy drains the legs.

**Say it out loud.** Written and spoken, your name is on it.

**Count what you have.** That's what the evening question is for.  
You push harder from a full tank.

Aim like it is everything. Release like it is nothing.  
Fist in. Open out.

**Full force. Open hand.**

---

## THE FEAST

All the steel is for one thing: the feast.  
Toughing it out is not the point. That's armor worn to bed.

> "Real joy, believe me, is a stern matter."  
> — Seneca, *Letters* 23.4

Once a day — the moment you sit down to eat — stop.  
Look at what's in front of you the way you'll remember it  
in ten years: a night you'd pay anything to stand in again.  
Say: *I'm standing in it now.*

At every goodbye with someone you love:  
*Mortal. Therefore precious. Therefore now.*  
Give the parting your whole face. No phone in the hand.

The feast is still on the table. Sit down to it.

---

## THE WHOLE LIFE

**AMBITION.** Two scoreboards: the win,  
and the man you were while you chased it.  
Lose the second and the medal is worthless.

**MONEY.** Make it without selling  
the mind that has to enjoy it.

**LOVE.** Yours to give: attention, honesty, repair.  
People go. Don't lose them early by being half-here.  
No verdicts while your face is hot.  
Fix it before pride sets.

**PEOPLE.** One useful thing each week for someone  
who can do nothing for you. Same day you read this through.

**THE NAME.** You are the fourth to carry it.  
A torch, not a chain.

**THE LONG GAME.** Can it be undone? Move now.  
If it can't, sleep first.  
The strongest man in the room is the one  
who won't become what the room rewards.

---

## THE RETURN

You missed a day. Or a week.  
Good — the book was built for this.  
Each time you come back to this page, it goes deeper.

Never miss twice. At the next bell, the smallest version.  
Ten seconds counts.

Count returns, not streaks.  
Chains are for prisoners.

Read it all once a week — same day, every week.

Day one is tonight, not Monday.  
Phone on the charger, two questions, done.  
Tomorrow your feet hit the floor  
and you already know the line.

**You are the kind of man who comes back.**

---

## THE CREED

*Aloud, once a week — the same sitting where you read it all.*  
*From memory by day thirty.*

I am Renato Zapata IV.  
The name behind me is inheritance.  
The life ahead of me is the assignment.  
I hold what is mine: my judgment, my aim, my next act.  
I release what is not: the outcome, the verdict, the past.  
I will not sign for every thought that knocks.  
I will love what is mortal harder for being mortal.  
I will not burn body, name, love, savings, or freedom for one hot hour.  
I will return after every fall — small, quick, no ceremony.  
I will guard the feast with steel, and sit down to it laughing.

Close the fist. Open the hand.

**Full force. Open hand.**

---

## THE LAST PAGE

> "Remember that in life you ought to behave as at a banquet. ...  
> Stretch out your hand and take a portion with decency.  
> Suppose that it passes by you. Do not detain it."  
> — Epictetus, *The Handbook* 15

The man behind the first handbook was a slave with a bad leg.  
He said his one job was to sing at his post and never leave it.  
Not enduring — rejoicing.

Most of what frightened you never came. What came, you carried.  
Fear shows up smaller than you pictured. The feast does not.

Do not be this book's reader. Be its evidence.  
That was always the aim: a true Stoic, a beautiful life.

Now close your fist — everything you will give.  
Open it — everything you cannot hold.

**The feast is still on the table. Spend it alive.**  
**Full force. Open hand.**

Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 2.1, 5.20, 6.6 (Hays); 5.16 (Hicks).

"The obstacle is the training" compresses Marcus;  
no ancient promised it.  
The Searchlight is modern psychology, not Stoicism —  
how attention, belief, and rehearsal change  
what you notice and what you do.  
Nothing here claims a thought moves the world.
