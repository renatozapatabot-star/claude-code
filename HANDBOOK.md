## <a id="p1"></a>PAGE 1 — YOU HAVE HAD A DRINK TONIGHT

Your judgement of how drunk you are was the first thing the drink took.

Decide nothing, send nothing, sign nothing, leave nothing. Tell no one tonight what you have been keeping: tonight is the wrong night to be believed, and it keeps till Thursday. What you meant to settle keeps too, and tomorrow you can think.

Delete what is written; do not reopen it — you need not see it again to delete it. If you cannot, close it and put the phone in another room. Hand it to someone only if that person has nothing to do with tonight.

The glow goes out when you hold it. Strip the appetite plain: wine is fermented grape juice. Take the measure the occasion allows, or none, and grip nothing.

One exception: if this is your wife, your child, the person you live with, read [page 8](#p8) before you sleep. Nothing on it asks anything of you tonight.

Failure: He said one drink; at the fourth he wrote to a woman he left years ago. He could have put the phone in another room at the second. So can you. Do it now.

## <a id="p2"></a>PAGE 2 — SOMEONE HAS DIED

Tears are a first movement — the body's shock, which no man chooses. Let them come, as long as they come. They stop on their own: not by clenching the jaw, and not by arguing with them.

He was mortal on the first day. Nobody asked you to agree to it, and knowing it now is not the same as having known it.

At three in the morning nothing is owed and nothing can be paid. The hour is not a failure; calling it one is a judgement you added. Write what the morning requires — paper, or the phone in your hand: one line. Then lie down in the dark whether or not you sleep. You are not required to sleep. You are required to stop.

For the first weeks you will work well and come apart in your own house. Both are the same man. Neither is the lie.

In daylight: get up and pay what the living are owed. One of them — the sister, the mother, the man who carried the coffin. Today.

What can still go wrong is the sentence passed afterward: that something of yours was taken. Nothing truly yours can be taken — but that is for the month after.

There is a sentence in Epictetus about what is lent and what is owed, *Enchiridion* XI. Do not go and find it tonight.

Months in, if the tears have become a rite — same hour, same chair — ask whether you grieve him or pay a debt. Not before then.

Failure: He meant to call his sister the day after the funeral. On the ninth day he still had not, and not calling had become a thing he was doing. He did not know what to say. You need nothing to say: "I couldn't call until now" is the whole call.

The phone is the last thing: type the line, turn it face down, leave it. **Stop here.** What is below will read tonight as contempt for your dead.

## <a id="p3"></a>PAGE 3 — SOMEONE HAS TOLD OTHER PEOPLE A THING ABOUT YOU THAT IS NOT TRUE

Something you did not do is now in the account other men keep of you. Correcting it is justice, not vanity. The urge is no weaker in the morning, and by then you are armed.

First: what actually happened — only what a stranger in the room could have written down? That is your material.

When is the decision taken? If not before you have slept, everything below holds. If the record closes today, you get one sentence, in writing, to whoever chairs it: the documentable fact, no adjective, no motive, no request. Then stop. Never more than one.

Until morning, one answer to anyone who raises it: "I'll come back to that with the dates." The same words each time. Never improvise a defence in a corridor; that version is the one people repeat.

The letter. Tomorrow morning, never this hour. One paragraph, not two. Facts with dates. No adjective about him, none about yourself. Nothing about his motive: if you cannot write it without a word about why he did it, it is not ready. Same distribution as the claim, no wider: if it was said aloud, the people in the room, on one message — not the team, not the minutes, not the next meeting.

If someone is accountable for you, tell him first, briefly, in the morning, before it goes out — same facts, no adjectives, not to enlist him but so he is not surprised.

Failure: The lie took him ten seconds. Your answer took four months, and by the second the room was watching you, not him. One paragraph, in the morning.

## <a id="p4"></a>PAGE 4 — SOMETHING HAS HAPPENED AND YOU ARE FRIGHTENED

Short breath, tight chest, the mind running one corridor. That is the body, not a verdict on your courage.

If you have done what the thing required and only the waiting is left, this is not your page — [page 5](#p5). A fear with nothing left to act on is neither cowardice nor neglect.

Name the feared thing in one plain sentence, no adjectives — paper, or the phone in your hand.

When it threatens money, comfort or standing, count what it can take: real losses, every one, none of them your character.

When it threatens the body or the years, do not count. The duty is what the hours until then require: the work, the people in the house, tonight's sleep. There is no other.

Where fear has postponed something justice required — the call, the disclosure, the payment — do it today. A fear you do not act on grows. If the hour forbids it, write its first line now and do it before noon.

Failure: He knew the accounts were false for two years and called it prudence. The two years began on a night like this. Write the first line now.

Failure: He put off the scan a year because he was afraid of the answer. The fear was not the cost; the year was. Make the call before noon.

## <a id="p5"></a>PAGE 5 — NOTHING HAS HAPPENED YET

You will wake at four and reach for the phone. That is the interval, not the news.

The result already exists — fixed before you opened this, in the sample, in the file, in the other man's decision. Nothing tonight moves it. What has not happened is your knowing it, and the knowing is what you have rehearsed forty times. That part is yours.

Find out the hour you will know. If no one has given you one, ask today; if everything is shut, write the question in a line and ask before nine.

Rehearsal is not preparation: you pay it once if it comes, not forty times first. Do not run the bad result through before you get up. Picturing a loss on purpose is a real exercise, done cold, in daylight, never while you wait on that loss. Four in the morning is not that: put your feet on the floor and begin the work.

Name what the interval requires — the work, the people in the house, the sleep — and do that. It is all you hold.

If someone in the house does not know, that conversation is owed, and it is daylight work. Tonight, write its first sentence.

Three in the morning is a fact about the hour, not about your case. Nothing is owed now and nothing can be paid. Write the one thing the morning requires — one line, paper or phone — then put this down and lie in the dark. Sleep is not required of you. Stopping is.

Failure: He spent the four days before the result dying of it; it came back clean and the four days were gone. Another man slept those days; his came back the other way, and the strength he kept was there when he needed it. Sleep is the work tonight.

## <a id="p6"></a>PAGE 6 — SHAKING, OR GOING OVER THE SAME MINUTE

The same minute, over and over, with the same ending. Your hands may shake for hours and the loop run all night. That is aftermath, not fresh vice — and not thinking either: nothing new has entered it since the fourth time round.

Two questions, on paper or phone.

What actually happened — only what a stranger in the room could have written down? A man said these words and left. There is a number on a page. A body is in a bed.

What did I add? The rest of it. All of it is yours.

Decide nothing tonight. The loop is one impression asking again for a verdict — an evil is here, something must be done now — and asking you to sign it. You have already refused. Do not crush the feeling and do not swallow it: feel it entirely, act on none of it.

Failure: His hands shook and he called himself guilty. The shaking was the body. The verdict was his, and it was wrong. Put the phone down and let it wear off.

## <a id="p7"></a>PAGE 7 — A MESSAGE YOU WANT TO SEND, OR A DOOR YOU WANT TO WALK OUT OF

Heat in the face, the speech already forming and forming well. It need not feel like anger: cold certainty at midnight is the same thing with the heat gone out. Your hands may shake for hours — aftermath, not fresh vice, and no evidence anything must be done now.

How long you wait is set by the size of the wound, not by how ready you feel. A word to a man's face: one breath. Anything written, sent or signed: not until you have slept. A public wrong or a betrayal: three days. A lie told about you runs on a shorter clock, because that record is closing while you wait — [page 3](#p3).

Anger is not the heat, which arrives on its own and which no man chooses. What you choose is the verdict you sign under it: an evil is here, something must be done now. Refuse the signature and the body still runs on; you answer for what you signed, and for what you did while it wore off.

No man chooses wrong knowing it is wrong: the one who injured you mistook it for good, and damaged himself.

> The best way of avenging thyself is not to become like the wrong doer.
>
> — *Meditations* VI.6 (Long)

Am I worse than I was an hour ago? If no, you were not corrupted; the rest is damage, none of it your character. If yes, name what you did: that is the damage, and the only part you can still stop. If a wrong was done, choose a consequence you can enforce cold, and enforce it in daylight.

Failure: He was unjust once; the other man was petty for a year. The year began with one message at this hour. He could have put the phone down. So can you.

## <a id="p8"></a>PAGE 8 — YOUR WIFE, YOUR CHILD, THE PERSON YOU LIVE WITH

This is someone you will still be living with on Thursday. Nothing won tonight is kept.

You repair nothing before you have slept. Repair is sober work, slower than you want, and attempted now it costs you the thing you are keeping.

Tonight: send nothing, say nothing further, sleep in the house, be in the kitchen in the morning. Standing there at seven is not the beginning of the repair. It is the repair. The words come later and are worth less.

Pass no verdict while your face is hot. Yours to give: attention, honesty, repair. Not yours: their choices, or their opinion of you tonight.

Failure: He was right, said so at midnight and in full, and was right alone for a year. He could have gone to bed and stood in the kitchen at seven. So can you.

## <a id="disciplines"></a>THE THREE DISCIPLINES

Epictetus taught three fields (*Discourses* III.2.1–2, Hard): desire and aversion, never to fail of what you desire nor fall into what you avoid; motive to act, orderly and reasoned; assent, freedom from hasty judgement. Name the field a failure came from, or you treat symptoms.

### Desire and Aversion

Indifferent does not mean unreal. Violence breaks bone, fraud takes the money, the grave takes her — none of it can make you worse without your consent.

One thing is good: excellence of character. One thing is evil: its absence. All else is indifferent in that sense only — health, wealth, reputation, the lives of your children, life itself. Seek not that things happen as you wish, but wish them as they are (*Enchiridion* VIII): not tolerate — wish.

> Never say about any thing, I have lost it, but say I have restored it. Is your child dead? It has been restored. Is your wife dead? She has been restored. Has your estate been taken from you? Has not then this also been restored?
>
> — *Enchiridion* XI (Long)

Not for the second week. He was lent, not owed.

### Action

Justice: give each man what the relation owes — wages, truth, attention; to a stranger, decency. The roles are assigned; selecting the part belongs to another (*Enchiridion* XVII).

> But suppose that he is a bad father. Were you then by nature made akin to a good father? No; but to a father.
>
> — *Enchiridion* XXX (Long)

The debt is fixed by the relation, not by the other man's conduct. Withdraw from a violent man; refuse a corrupt order; his failure is not your permission, and yours is not excused by his.

Failure: Your son asked you four times and you never looked up. He has stopped asking. Ask him something tomorrow, with the phone in another room.

### Assent

> Men are disturbed not by the things which happen, but by the opinions about the things: for example, death is nothing terrible, for if it were, it would have seemed so to Socrates; for the opinion about death, that it is terrible, is the terrible thing.
>
> — *Enchiridion* V (Long)

The impression arrives on its own; the heart jumps, the face heats. The first shock, which no man chooses, is not yet the passion (*On Anger* II.2–4). The passion is an impulse run past reason, carrying a verdict — an evil is here, something must be done now. It wants your signature. Refuse it and the body still runs on; you answer for what you signed, and for what you did while it wore off.

One thing is walled off: *prohairesis*, your power to assent, refuse, desire, act. Only you can reach it, and you surrender it daily.

Failure: You had the word for it by the second hour, and signed anyway by the third.

## <a id="practice"></a>DAILY PRACTICE

Before you get up, rehearse the men: the busy-body, the ungrateful, the arrogant, the deceitful. They are so through ignorance of good and evil; we are made for co-operation, like feet, like hands (*Meditations* II.1).

Then rehearse the loss (*Enchiridion* XXI): once, cold, by choice — picture it, then do the duty it would have interrupted. That is the exercise; what runs by itself and ends in nothing is not.

Before sleep, aloud, hide nothing (*On Anger* III.36). What did I do badly? Name the act, not the feeling. What did I do well? Name it once and move on. When it comes again, what do I do instead? Say the replacement in full. Then close it: be accurate, not sorry.

Then the four. Wise: did I see what was there, or what I feared and wanted? Just: did I give each man what was owed? Courageous: did I do what reason required, despite the fear? Temperate: did I take what the occasion asked, all or none, and grip nothing?

A no names a field. Train there tomorrow, run the four after any disturbance, and shorten how long you stay wrong.

## <a id="forge"></a>THE FORGE

Not in the first months: do the morning and evening for a season first. Nobody around you knows the word Stoic; they notice you are hard to move and pay what you owe.

> Set aside a certain number of days, during which you shall be content with the scantiest and cheapest fare, with coarse and rough dress, saying to yourself the while: Is this the condition that I feared?
>
> — Seneca, *Letters* XVIII.5–6 (Gummere)

Once a week, something of real use for a man who cannot repay you, arranged so he never learns. Catch yourself composing the account and you have taken your payment.

Failure: You took cold baths for sixty days and made sure three people knew. Do the next sixty and tell nobody.

You came here to feel less. That is honest, and not what this trains: relief comes as a byproduct or not at all. If being seen was the reward, you trained reputation, not character. If you can quote more than you can do, you are collecting: wisdom is what stands at the counter. If this made you colder toward people, you misread every page.

## <a id="burn"></a>WHEN TO BURN THIS

Put it in a drawer for a month; if the practice degrades, you were leaning on the object.

When that has held for a season — not a week — copy out by hand what changed your conduct. Not this book's sentences. Theirs: *Enchiridion* V, VIII, XI, XVII, XXX; *Meditations* VI.6; the four questions.

Then destroy this copy. The mechanics were disposable and carry no authority. Quote the masters or say nothing.

## <a id="last"></a>THE LAST PAGE

Read this in a calm hour, not a frightened one.

You are going to die, and the date is fixed. The body, the office, the name, the money, the house, the people at the table: all lent. Build well anyway. Whether anyone remembers the work was never yours. How you did it, and why, was.

One question: did I keep what was mine and return what was not?

Close the book. When you no longer need it, the handbook has succeeded.
